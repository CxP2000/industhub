Connection solution for high-pressure leak testing of flared tubes in refrigeration compressors

Flared ports on air conditioner and refrigerator compressors need frequent fast connection while withstanding high-pressure air-tightness testing. Gripping, sealing and flow path design are all essential.

The connection challenge of flared ports

Background

On refrigeration production lines, compressor air-tightness and pressure testing are key to product quality. Most small refrigeration compressors for air conditioners and refrigerators use flared tube ports. How to connect and seal them quickly during air-tightness or pressure testing directly affects test efficiency and line takt.

Gripping and sealing structure

Sealing

Dedicated connectors for small compressor flared tubes use a 316 stainless steel body with good pressure and corrosion resistance, suited to high-frequency, long-term line use. Stainless steel jaws grip the raised face of the flared tube end directly; this external-clamp jaw design gives enough grip to stay locked under pressure fluctuation or gas surge.

The seal uses a double-layer self-adjusting expansion design: under high pressure the seals conform more tightly as pressure changes, meeting test needs from vacuum to 70 Bar and keeping a stable seal in high-pressure air-tightness testing.

Operation, flow path and maintenance

Operation

The connector uses a sliding sleeve: align with the tube and push to lock and seal, pull the sleeve to disconnect, with no tools or complex training. The straight-through internal flow path minimises pressure loss and flow decay of the test media (compressed air, helium or refrigerant), which matters where charge pressure or flow must be controlled precisely and helps keep results true and reliable.

Routine maintenance is mainly periodic seal replacement, quick and simple; the robust body needs no frequent part replacement, helping control long-term station operating cost.
