Routine Maintenance for Gas Cylinder Filling Connectors

Regular checks of seals and safety mechanisms are the cheapest way to keep filling stations reliably connected.

News Company News Case Studies FAQ

Set up daily, weekly and periodic checks

Inspection

Gas filling connectors are used long-term for fast filling of compressed gas and air-tightness testing of compressed cylinders, and need periodic maintenance to give a reliable connection every time. The following guidelines apply to all filling connectors.

Operators should carry out daily, weekly and periodic checks covering connector wear, body damage, tightness, ease of operation, lubrication, dirt build-up and damage. Non-specialists should not disassemble the connector.

Seals: replace every 2,000 cycles, inspect daily

Seals

Replace the main O-ring seal at least every 2,000 cycles and visually inspect its surface daily; replace it if torn or cracked. Some applications need more frequent seal replacement.

The seal is the main wear part of a filling connector; writing the replacement interval into the station checklist is cheaper than stopping to troubleshoot after a leak appears.

Poppet and safety lift ring

Ejector & Ring

For connectors with a poppet, check before daily use that it is sound and not bent; if bent or damaged, contact the manufacturer for original spare parts.

Check the safety lift ring periodically: tighten loose screws moderately but do not overtighten; confirm the ring is in the correct position and adjust per the troubleshooting guide if not, or return it to the manufacturer if the problem persists.

Safety latch: only unlock after depressurising

Safety Lock

Check the safety latch daily. On lift-ring gas filling connectors, the latch drops automatically when the safety lift ring is released and can only be pressed to unlock after the connector is depressurised. Never try to disconnect under pressure, or the connector may be damaged and personal or property safety put at risk.
