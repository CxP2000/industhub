Leak Testing Automotive Hoses: Quick Sealing for Low-Pressure Hoses

Hoses differ from rigid tubes because clamping deforms them; solve the deformation first, then decide between manual and pneumatic.

Why hoses are harder to seal than rigid tubes

Challenge

Vehicles and their components contain many lines carrying coolant, oil and other media, and they also need leak testing before shipment. The key difference between hose parts and metal rigid tubes is that clamping during leak testing causes substantial deformation.

Once deformed, an ordinary quick seal connector cannot hold, causing seal failure or unstable test results. Hose testing therefore cannot reuse rigid-tube clamping logic and needs a design that addresses deformation.

Stop sleeve: solve hose deformation first

Solution

To address deformation in low-pressure hoses, add a stop sleeve on top of the inner seal: an outer rigid structure limits radial deformation during clamping so the internal seal still contacts the tube wall for a stable seal.

This is the core difference between hose and rigid-tube leak testing connections: control deformation first, then discuss clamping force and pressure rating.

Manual stations: G25A with a rigid housing

Manual

Fit a rigid housing over the G25A and it works as a manual quick seal connector, fine for ordinary low-pressure lines up to 3 kg.

G25A features: manual operation, within 3 bar, suited to most water and oil circuits. For stations with modest takt requirements and manual loading.

Automated stations: G15 pneumatic sealing

Pneumatic

G15 is a pneumatic seal connector; with the same rigid housing it suits automated pneumatic sealing and handles volume production well.

G15 features: pneumatic automatic operation, within 3 bar, suited to most water and oil circuits. The choice between manual and pneumatic mainly depends on whether the station needs automatic loading and the line takt.
