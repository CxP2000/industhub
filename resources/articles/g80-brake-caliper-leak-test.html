Connector selection for brake caliper leak testing

Caliper air-tightness testing covers vacuum, low pressure and high pressure stages. Pressure margin, actuation, neck length and serviceability are the four key selection dimensions.

Why calipers must be leak tested

Background

The brake caliper directly executes braking in the vehicle braking system; its internal piston chamber withstands high pressure, high temperature and frequent friction over time. If sealing is inadequate, brake fluid may leak, reducing braking force, lengthening stopping distance and in extreme cases causing brake failure. Industry testing usually covers vacuum sealing, low-pressure sealing and high-pressure sealing, each with strict limits on pressure retention and leakage. Whether testing runs smoothly largely depends on the quality of the quick seal connector's connection to the threaded hole.

Four selection dimensions

Selection

Pressure capability: caliper leak testing involves charging and holding high-pressure gas, with large pressure differences between stages, so the connector's rated pressure must leave margin to avoid leakage or deformation across the whole test pressure range.

Actuation: manual assembly lines need lever connectors with intuitive operation and clear feel; semi- or fully automated stations suit pneumatic actuation for easy integration with control systems; in between, a button-triggered semi-automatic option works. Choose by the line's actual automation level to avoid efficiency loss from a connector-station mismatch.

Space adaptability: caliper bodies are complex and often surrounded by sensors and fixtures; if neck length is not flexible, the connector may not reach the threaded hole smoothly in tight space. Multiple neck lengths are key to handling structural interference.

Serviceability: in volume production connectors are used at high frequency, and seals and jaws are wear parts. Quick-change face seals and standardised internal component replacement directly affect line downtime and overall efficiency.

How the high-pressure internal-thread series responds

Solution

Internal-thread quick seal connectors for high-pressure internal-thread testing withstand up to 350 Bar, covering caliper testing from low-pressure sealing to high-pressure withstand; the 316 stainless steel construction ensures mechanical strength and wear resistance under frequent mating, avoiding seal failure from structural deformation.

Modular actuation is the focus: lever drive (G80L) suits manual lines with a clear locking feel; pneumatic drive (G80P) integrates into semi- or fully automated stations controlled by pneumatic signals; button drive (G80V) suits manual triggering with less operator effort. All three modules swap quickly on the same body, so line upgrades need only a new drive module rather than replacing the whole connector, cutting retrofit cost and spare parts inventory.

Multiple neck lengths are available, with some models at 12.5 mm or 25.5 mm so the body clears nearby obstacles. For maintenance, the front face seal is easy to replace and its material can be chosen by media: NBR for routine air-tightness testing, FKM or other chemical-resistant materials when brake fluid or special oils are involved; some jaws have flow holes to shorten test time, and standardised internal components can be replaced as a set when worn, without complex on-site disassembly and adjustment.
